## Core Specifications
- **MCU**: ESP32-S3FH4R2 (Xtensa® 32-bit LX7 dual-core, up to 240MHz)
- **Memory**: 4MB Flash, 2MB PSRAM
- **Wireless**: 2.4GHz Wi-Fi, Bluetooth 5 (LE)
- **USB**: Type-C (Native USB, no USB-to-UART chip)
- **RGB LED**: WS2812 connected to **GPIO21**

::tip
The USB port is native USB, not a USB-to-UART chip. In the Arduino IDE, enable **USB CDC On Boot** or `Serial.println()` never reaches the cable.
::

## Power
- **Input**: 3.7 V to 6 V on the 5V pad, or 5 V from USB-C.
- **Current**: At least 500 mA when powered from the 5V pad.
- **3V3 pad**: Output of the onboard LDO, up to 800 mA. This is the rail for 3.3 V sensors. Do not feed 5 V into it.
- **Size**: 18.0 mm × 23.5 mm, castellated pads on a 2.54 mm pitch.

## Pinout

---
src: esp32-s3-zero-pinout.jpg
alt: ESP32-S3-Zero pinout, front and back
height: 30rem
---
::

USB-C sits at the top of the front. Pads down the left edge are GPIO1–GPIO6. Pads down the right edge are TX, RX, then GPIO13, GPIO12, GPIO11, GPIO10, GPIO9, GPIO8, GPIO7, GPIO16, GPIO15, and GPIO14.

The back edge brings out GPIO45, GPIO42, GPIO41, GPIO40, GPIO39, GPIO38, GPIO18, and GPIO17.

| Pad | What it is |
| :--- | :--- |
| **5V, GND, 3V3** | Power in, ground, and 3.3 V out |
| **TX / RX** | UART0 on GPIO43 / GPIO44 |
| **GPIO1–GPIO10** | ADC1-capable. GPIO1 and GPIO2 are the sensor-hub UART |
| **GPIO11–GPIO18** | ADC2-capable, except GPIO19 and GPIO20, which are not broken out |
| **GPIO21** | WS2812 data. Onboard LED, not a castellated pad |
| **GPIO38–GPIO42, GPIO45** | Back-edge GPIO. GPIO45 is a strapping pin |

GPIO19 and GPIO20 are the native USB pins and stay on the connector. The flash and the 2 MB PSRAM sit on an internal bus, so they do not occupy any pad. GPIO33–GPIO37 are not brought out either. This is quad PSRAM, not the octal bus those pins would serve on an 8 MB part.

::caution
GPIO0, GPIO3, and GPIO45 are strapping pins. GPIO0 low at power-on is download mode. GPIO45 pulled high at reset can stop the module booting, because it selects the flash voltage. They are fine as GPIO once the chip is running.
::

## Buttons
- **BOOT** (GPIO0): hold while plugging in USB to enter the ROM download mode.
- **RESET**: restarts the chip. It is not a GPIO.

## How the running boards are wired
The nodes already in use follow two pages:

- [mmWave presence](/iot/mcus/esp32/esp32-s3-zero/mmwave-integration) — UART at 256000 baud. The first hookup used UART0 (GPIO43/44). Later boards move that UART to GPIO1/GPIO2 so USB logs stay free.
- [Sensor hub](/iot/mcus/esp32/esp32-s3-zero/sensor-hub) — that GPIO1/GPIO2 UART, plus BME280 and BH1750 on I²C GPIO8/GPIO9, a status LED on GPIO4, and buttons on GPIO5 and GPIO6.

The matching ESPHome template is [ESP32-S3-Zero ESPHome Config](/iot/firmware/esphome/esp32-zero-sample).
