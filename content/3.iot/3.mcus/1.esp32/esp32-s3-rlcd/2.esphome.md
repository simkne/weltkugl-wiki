# ESPHome on the ESP32-S3-RLCD-4.2

With ESPHome on this board it is a local display and voice node. The use cases below follow Waveshare's [ESP32-S3-RLCD-4.2 configuration example](https://docs.waveshare.com/ESP32-ESPHome-Tutorials/Example-RLCD-Voice). Each one stands alone: flash the display, add climate and battery, then add the speaker and microphones only if you want them.

Pins are the ones on the [hardware reference](/iot/mcus/esp32/esp32-s3-rlcd/hardware-ref). A ready-made config is also listed on [devices.esphome.io](https://devices.esphome.io/) under `ESP32-S3-RLCD-4.2`.

## Shared base

Two settings sit under every example:

- **Octal PSRAM at 80 MHz.** The 8 MB PSRAM holds the display framebuffer and the audio buffers. Without `psram: mode: octal`, the firmware can compile and still crash on boot.
- **ST7305 driver** from [`kylehase/ESPHome-ST7305-RLCD`](https://github.com/kylehase/ESPHome-ST7305-RLCD), component `st7305_rlcd`. It is not in ESPHome mainline yet. Keep the framework on `esp-idf`.

Keep the API key, OTA password, and device name that the ESPHome wizard generates. Replacing the whole file with someone else's YAML drops those and Home Assistant pairing fails.

## Display

The panel is an ST7305 reflective LCD, 400×300, with no backlight. SPI is write-only: clock GPIO11, data GPIO12, chip select GPIO40, data/command GPIO5, reset GPIO41. `data_rate: 1MHz` is enough. A refresh every 30–60 seconds is plenty; the image stays on the glass between updates.

A first flash can draw a test card to prove the link. After that, a display lambda paints a title bar, temperature, humidity, and a battery bar. The display is a canvas, not a Home Assistant entity.

## Climate, battery, and buttons

SHTC3, the audio codecs, and the RTC share one I²C bus on GPIO13 (SDA) and GPIO14 (SCL). The SHTC3 is at `0x70` (`shtcx`). A bus scan at boot should also show the ES8311 at `0x18` and the ES7210 at `0x40` or `0x42`.

The 18650 is read on GPIO4 (ADC1 channel 3) through a 3× divider. Set the ADC attenuation to `12db` and multiply the reading by 3. Map 2.5 V to 0% and 4.2 V to 100%.

BOOT (GPIO0) and KEY (GPIO18) are active-low inputs.

## Voice

The speaker (ES8311) and the dual microphones (ES7210) share one I²S bus: bit clock GPIO9, word select GPIO45, master clock GPIO16, speaker data GPIO8, microphone data GPIO10. GPIO46 enables the amplifier. Leave it low and the speaker stays silent.

Three checks, from a local tone up to speech:

1. **Ringtone.** `rtttl` plays a melody from a Home Assistant button. The tune is synthesized on the device, so it needs no audio file and no network.
2. **Speech.** Wrap the speaker as a media player and Home Assistant can read text aloud. The board fetches that audio from Home Assistant's internal URL, so the URL has to be plain `http://` on the LAN IP, port 8123. A `.local` name or `https://` with a self-signed certificate times out.
3. **Wake word.** `micro_wake_word` listens on the ESP32 for "Hey Jarvis" and bumps a counter on the display. It needs `espressif/esp-nn`. This does not use Home Assistant's voice pipeline.

The PCF85063 RTC and the TF card slot are on the board, and this example leaves both unused.

## When something stays quiet

- **No ringtone.** Speaker enable on GPIO46 is off.
- **Speech fails** with an HTTP or TLS timeout. Home Assistant's internal URL is wrong. Local ringtones still work, because they are compiled into the firmware.
- **Wake word never fires.** `esp-nn` is missing, or the microphone gain is too low.
- **Battery stuck at 0.** Attenuation is not `12db`, or the ×3 scale is missing.
